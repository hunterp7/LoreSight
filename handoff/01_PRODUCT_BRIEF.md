# Product Brief

## Working names

- Platform/language: **Storyframe**
- First world: **The Agency**
- First case: **Applicant Intake**

Storyframe is a working name and has not received trademark clearance.

## Opportunity

Most AI story experiences are either static branching fiction with an AI coat of paint or unconstrained chat roleplay that loses causality and canon. Storyframe occupies the middle:

- authored narrative meaning;
- deterministic mechanics and consequences;
- bounded AI interpolation;
- personalized performance;
- visible creator control;
- replayable, testable state.

## Primary users

### Creator

A writer, designer, game master, fan community, educator, or small studio that has strong ideas but may not want to program state machines.

The creator wants to:

- describe a premise naturally;
- lock characters and canon;
- control critical moments, choices, reveals, and endings;
- let AI develop connective material;
- understand what the engine inferred;
- playtest and revise without losing control.

### Player

A player who wants consequential interactive fiction that feels responsive and personal but remains coherent over time.

## Core product loop

### Creator

1. Begin with an evocative premise.
2. Answer the narrative questions that shape the experience.
3. Watch characters, canon, threads, critical frames, spans, and endings appear in a living story spine.
4. Review **What the engine understood**.
5. Approve the semantic contract.
6. Playtest any branch.
7. Annotate authored and generated material.
8. Review the canon delta.
9. Publish an immutable world version.

### Player

1. Enter a world through ChatGPT or a standalone client.
2. Observe a situation.
3. choose through free-form language or visible actions.
4. Receive deterministic consequences and AI-performed narrative.
5. Review characters, places, clues, objects, and timeline.
6. Continue until an ending or continuation state.

## Product principles

- Meaning is authored; connective performance can be generated.
- The creator approves semantics, not implementation details.
- The Studio asks only questions whose answers materially change the game.
- Mystery and negative space are protected features.
- Generated details have explicit persistence scopes.
- The player may know, believe, suspect, or be wrong; those are different states.
- Every important consequence must be explainable from the event log.
- A world must remain playable when AI generation fails.

## Version-one boundaries

Include:

- single-player, turn-based narratives;
- Storyframe compiler;
- flags, knowledge, clues, resources, clocks, inventory, relationships, locations, routes, seeded checks, markets, endings;
- Creator Studio critical-moment and span editing;
- character recap;
- ChatGPT player app;
- Agency conformance world;
- survival and trading synthetic worlds;
- durable saves.

Exclude:

- real-time action or physics;
- multiplayer;
- public marketplace;
- automatic publishing;
- unrestricted third-party IP distribution;
- complex 3D scenes;
- autonomous promotion of generated content into canon.

## Intellectual-property boundary

Public and monetized worlds must be original, public-domain, or licensed. A private user-supplied remix designation may constrain distribution but does not create legal permission or a fair-use guarantee.
